# Human Review Checklist — EU AI Act Compliance File (Simulated Case)

> **SIMULATION NOTICE.** This project is a fictional compliance file created for
> portfolio and educational purposes. Not legal advice.

## Purpose

This checklist is applied to **every artifact** in this repository before it is
considered final (and before publication to GitHub). The artifacts are
AI-assisted drafts reviewed by the author; in a real organisation each would
additionally need review by qualified counsel and the relevant business owners.

Adapted from the human review checklist of the author's previous project
(NGO Policy Briefing Agent).

---

## 1. Simulation integrity

- [ ] Simulation notice and "not legal advice" header present.
- [ ] All company names, data, and events trace back to `case/case-definition.md`.
- [ ] No case facts invented beyond the case definition; deviations are labelled
      as Assumptions or fed back into the case definition.
- [ ] Weaknesses in the case evidence surface as findings and are not
      silently resolved.

Reviewer notes:

## 2. Legal citation accuracy

- [ ] Every legal claim cites a specific article/paragraph/annex point.
- [ ] Citations spot-checked against the official text (artificialintelligenceact.eu
      or EUR-Lex) — at minimum every citation that carries a conclusion.
- [ ] Binding text distinguished from recitals, guidance, and commentary.
- [ ] Timeline claims give both original and Digital Omnibus-revised dates, and
      the Omnibus adoption status was verified by web search on the date stated.
- [ ] Anything unverified is marked **[unverified]**.

Reviewer notes:

## 3. Evidence discipline

- [ ] Fact (case), Fact (law), Interpretation, Assumption, Recommendation,
      Uncertainty, and Evidence gap are distinguished per
      `methodology/evidence-rules.md`.
- [ ] No interpretation is presented as settled law.
- [ ] Confidence levels used where a reviewer would otherwise have to guess.
- [ ] Ambiguity in the law is stated, not resolved by convenience.

Reviewer notes:

## 4. Rights-holder focus

- [ ] Risks to **people** (candidates, workers) are stated separately from
      risks to the **organisation**, and neither is collapsed into the other.
- [ ] Affected groups are described accurately and respectfully, without
      stereotyping.
- [ ] Contestability, remedy, and information rights of candidates are
      addressed, not just employer-side process.

Reviewer notes:

## 5. Recommendations

Each recommendation states:

- [ ] who should act (named role);
- [ ] what concrete action is proposed;
- [ ] why (obligation, risk, or gap it answers);
- [ ] which evidence supports it;
- [ ] what uncertainty remains;
- [ ] whether it is realistic and proportionate for the actor named.

Reviewer notes:

## 6. Internal consistency across artifacts

- [ ] Classifications in Artifact 01 match roles/classifications used in 02–04.
- [ ] Risks in the register (02) align with FRIA-Plus findings (03) and
      vendor red flags (04); discrepancies are explained.
- [ ] Terminology (system name, role labels, band names) is identical across
      artifacts.

Reviewer notes:

## 7. Tone and publication readiness

- [ ] Plain professional English; no hype, no filler.
- [ ] Reads as a working compliance document, not an essay.
- [ ] No real personal data, no real company implicated.
- [ ] Suitable for public GitHub publication as a portfolio piece.

Reviewer decision:

- [ ] Final — ready for publication
- [ ] Ready after minor edits
- [ ] Needs major revision
- [ ] Not suitable
